# 4. Medical Content SEO – 10 Article Briefs

## How to use these briefs (E-E-A-T rules for health pages)
Google treats medical pages as **YMYL** ("Your Money or Your Life"), so it holds them to the highest quality bar. To meet it:
1. **Dr. Bibin Baby writes or reviews every article himself.** Show "Written / medically reviewed by Dr. Bibin Baby, MS ENT, DNB ENT, Reg. No. …" and a "Last reviewed" date.
2. **Add experience** that no AI or competitor can copy: what patients in Pala commonly ask, seasonal patterns you see (monsoon colds, rubber-pollen season), and how a visit actually works in your clinic. Never use identifiable cases.
3. **Cite guidelines** (listed per article). Link to the original source.
4. **Include red flags**: when to go to a hospital urgently.
5. **No promises:** no "cure", "permanent", "painless", "100%", "best".
6. **Length:** 1,000–1,800 words, plus a 3–4 line Malayalam summary at the top.
7. **NMC (Oct 2026):** educational content is allowed. Don't add patient testimonials, before/after images or outcome statistics from your own practice framed as promotion.

## 4.1 Sinusitis
- **URL:** `/sinusitis/`
- **SEO title:** Sinusitis: Symptoms, Causes & Treatment in Pala | Dr. Bibin Baby
- **Meta description:** Blocked nose, facial pressure and thick discharge for weeks? Learn about acute and chronic sinusitis, when antibiotics help, and when to see an ENT in Pala.
- **Primary keyword:** sinusitis treatment in Pala
- **Secondary:** sinus specialist Pala, chronic sinusitis, sinus headache, FESS Kottayam, nasal polyps
- **Search intent:** Informational → commercial (deciding whether to see a specialist)
- **H1:** Sinusitis (Sinus Infection): Symptoms, Causes and Treatment
- **Outline:**
  - H2 What is sinusitis?
    - H3 Acute vs chronic sinusitis (acute: under 4 weeks; chronic: 12 weeks or more)
  - H2 Common symptoms
  - H2 Is it a cold or sinusitis? *(most colds are viral and settle within 10 days)*
  - H2 What causes sinusitis? (viral infection, allergy, deviated septum, polyps, dental causes)
  - H2 When should you see an ENT doctor?
    - H3 Red flags: go to hospital urgently
  - H2 How sinusitis is diagnosed (history, nasal endoscopy, CT only when needed)
  - H2 Treatment options
    - H3 Home care and saline irrigation
    - H3 Medicines (nasal steroid sprays; antibiotics only when bacterial infection is likely)
    - H3 When surgery (FESS) is considered
  - H2 FAQs
- **Patient-friendly opening:**
  > Your sinuses are small air spaces in the bones around your nose and eyes. When their lining swells, mucus can't drain, and you feel blocked, heavy-headed and congested. This is sinusitis. Most cases start with a cold and get better on their own within 10 days. When symptoms last longer, keep coming back, or continue for more than three months, an ENT examination helps find the reason and the right treatment.
- **Red flags:** swelling or redness around the eye, double vision or reduced vision, severe headache with stiff neck, confusion, high fever with facial swelling.
- **FAQs:**
  1. *Do I need antibiotics for sinusitis?* Usually not. Most acute sinusitis is viral. Antibiotics are considered when symptoms last more than 10 days without improvement, are severe, or get worse again after initial improvement.
  2. *Is a CT scan always needed?* No. CT is used for chronic sinusitis, planning surgery, or suspected complications.
  3. *Can sinusitis be cured permanently?* Many people get good long-term control. Chronic sinusitis can recur, especially with allergy or polyps, so ongoing care may be needed.
  4. *Does steam help?* Steam may feel soothing. Saline nasal irrigation has better evidence. Avoid very hot steam, especially with children, because of the risk of burns.
- **Internal links:** Deviated septum, Allergic rhinitis, Nasal polyps, FESS procedure, Pala location
- **References:** Rosenfeld RM et al. *Clinical Practice Guideline (Update): Adult Sinusitis.* Otolaryngol Head Neck Surg. 2015. · Fokkens WJ et al. *EPOS 2020: European Position Paper on Rhinosinusitis and Nasal Polyps.* Rhinology. 2020.
